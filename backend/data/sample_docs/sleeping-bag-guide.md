# Choosing a Sleeping Bag

## Understanding temperature ratings

Sleeping bags carry a comfort rating and a limit rating. The comfort rating is the lowest temperature at which a cold-sleeping person can still sleep comfortably. The limit rating is the lowest temperature at which a warm-sleeping person can sleep without shivering, curled up. Choose a bag whose comfort rating is at or just below the coldest night you expect, and add a few degrees of margin if you sleep cold. A sleeping pad matters as much as the bag, because the ground steals heat from underneath you.

## Down or synthetic

Down is lighter, packs smaller and lasts for decades if cared for, but it loses its insulation when wet and costs more. Synthetic insulation is heavier and bulkier, yet it keeps most of its warmth when damp and dries quickly, which suits wet climates and budget buyers. Our down bags use water-resistant treated down that tolerates light condensation, though it should still be kept dry.

## Shape and fit

Mummy bags are the warmest for their weight because they leave little empty space to heat. Rectangular bags give more room to move but are heavier and colder. A bag should fit snugly without compressing the insulation. If you are taller than 6 feet 2 inches, choose the long size.

## Washing and storage

Wash a sleeping bag no more than once a season, using a gentle front-loading washer and a cleaner made for down or synthetic fill. Dry it on low heat with a few clean tennis balls to break up clumps. Store the bag uncompressed in the large cotton storage sack included with it, never in its small stuff sack.

## Packing tips

Stuff the bag into its sack without folding, starting with the foot end. Folding the same way every time creates creases that wear down the insulation over the years.
