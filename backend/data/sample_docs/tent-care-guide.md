# Tent Care and Waterproofing Guide

## Setting up and taking down

Choose a flat spot free of sharp stones and sticks, and lay a footprint or groundsheet under the tent floor to protect it from abrasion. Stake out the corners first, then raise the poles and clip the tent body on. Always tension the rainfly so that it does not touch the inner tent, because contact lets water wick through the fabric. Take the tent down gently and never pull stakes out by yanking on the cord loops.

## Drying and storage

A wet tent will grow mildew within a couple of days and mildew permanently damages waterproof coatings. When you get home, pitch the tent in the yard or hang it up indoors until every seam and the floor is completely dry. Store it loosely in a large breathable sack in a cool dry place. Do not keep a tent compressed in its stuff sack all winter, and never store it in a hot attic or car trunk, because heat breaks down the coating.

## Cleaning

Clean the tent with lukewarm water and a soft sponge. Do not put a tent in a washing machine or dryer, and avoid detergents and household cleaners, which strip the waterproof layer. For stubborn tree sap, dab a little rubbing alcohol on a cloth. Lubricate sticky zippers with a zipper lubricant and brush grit out of the teeth with an old toothbrush.

## Re-waterproofing

If water stops beading on the rainfly and starts soaking into the fabric, it is time to reproof. Set up the tent, wipe it clean and spray on a fabric waterproofing treatment, following the label. Seams that leak can be resealed with seam sealer, applied to the inside of the fly and the floor and left to cure for 12 hours. Reproofing every two or three seasons keeps a tent weatherproof for years.

## Field repairs

Carry a pole repair sleeve and a few adhesive fabric patches. A cracked pole segment can be splinted with the sleeve and tape, and small tears in the fly can be patched from the outside once the fabric is dry.
